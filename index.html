The cache only pays when the prompt holds still

A serving-cost audit usually starts with the token price list. The next line down is the cache line. Put a 3,000 token system kit in front of every call and the bill depends on one habit: whether calls repeat within five minutes. A day of forty calls spread across the calendar never trips the clock, so every call pays list: 120,000 input tokens at $3.00 a million is 36 cents a day, $131.40 a year, for the privilege of re-reading identical instructions.

Group the same forty calls so each five minute window holds one fresh call and nine hits. The window costs 0.9 cents fresh plus 0.81 cents cached, four windows a day comes to $24.97 a year, an 81 percent cut without touching the kit. Trimming the kit to 800 tokens instead gets 73 percent. Doing both lands at $6.66 a year, 95 percent under the scattered baseline, and the quality the kit buys stays on the bill.

The cache line breaks from the editing side. Caching is prefix work: change one line of the kit and everything after it re-prices at list. Six tweaks a day plus scattered calls hands the whole discount back, the day drifts to full price, and the $131.40 stands. The rule is short: the prompt that holds still earns the discount, and the team that iterates copy at noon pays for the iteration in tokens.
